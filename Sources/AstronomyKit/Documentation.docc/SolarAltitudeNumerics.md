# Solar altitude: numerical accuracy

Derived partial arithmetic bounds and independent measurements for the native Sun altitude calculation.

## Scope and reference

``Sun/altitudeObservation(universalTime:from:deltaTModel:)``, ``Sun/altitudeObservation(terrestrialTime:from:deltaTModel:)`` and ``Sun/altitudeObservation(at:from:deltaTModel:)`` construct native model time and return the Sun's geometric altitude with the recorded UT, TT, observer and named Delta T model. Repeating ordinary `CelestialBody.sun.horizon(at: observation.time, from: observation.observer, refraction: .none)` returns the same altitude bits. The observation's ``SolarAltitudeObservation/ErrorBound`` is a **partial numerical budget**, not a bound on every numerical error or on the difference between the model and the sky.

The model remains the shipped Earth polynomial fit to VSOP87B, its documented fixed EQJ transformation, IAU 2006 precession, IAU 2006/2000A nutation with all 33+1 complementary equation-of-equinoxes terms, the IERS observer ellipsoid, and the existing light-time/first-order-aberration calculation. The named Delta T choices are Espenak–Meeus with the native continuous Gregorian decimal year in this coverage, and the library's JPL Horizons approximation that holds that model at UT `17 × 365.24217`. These choices describe different model inputs, not uncertainty bounds. Published JPL Horizons and EclipseWise comparisons retain their existing tolerances.

The exact reference starts from the supplied scalar. A UT or TT `Double` is its exact binary64 value. A `Date` is its stored binary64 seconds since 2001-01-01; exact civil days are those seconds divided by 86400, plus 365.5. Before the civil table starts in 1961, civil days are the historical UT1 proxy. Thereafter the exact table formula supplies TT. A TT input uses a mathematical inverse of the chosen Delta T model; where two UT solutions overlap, the recorded UT identifies the selected solution. A positive model gap has no reference UT and is refused. Light-time uses the same ten-evaluation, strict `1e-9`-day stopping rule in exact arithmetic. It is not replaced with an always-existing fixed point or an arbitrary tenth-iterate result.

## Reproducible artifacts

`Scripts/solar-numerics/numerics.py` derives the Earth speed, radius and joins in exact rational arithmetic. `certify_light_time.py` uses outward rational intervals and integer-square-root enclosures to certify successful reference return on the finite exceptional public-input sets. `derive_altitude.py` parses the native expressions and tables, derives the partial budget and classification guards, and generates `SolarAltitudeBounds.swift`. The JSON artifacts record rational values, outward binary64 values where applicable, canonical coverage and source/data hashes. Their check modes reconstruct the results; editing a report or deleting a required strip does not reduce the checked domain.

`reference.py` and `solar_reference.py` independently evaluate the published model definitions with mpmath 1.3.0 at 80 decimal digits. Earth polynomials use the defining Chebyshev sum, rather than native Clenshaw evaluation; the full VSOP series is used for sampled fallback epochs. Precession is composed as elementary rotations, and altitude is computed by vector projection without the native right-ascension/declination round trip. Existing SOFA/ERFA and IMCCE definition checks and selected 80/110-digit convergence tests accompany the evaluator. Precision convergence is evidence about those samples, not a transcendental-function certificate. The pinned SOFA/VI42 reference-fixture generation and integrity checks remain separate and unchanged.

Source hashes identify evaluated files, not mathematical validity by themselves. They catch stale artifacts after a bound expression or data file changes. Regenerating a hash cannot establish that a new expression satisfies the old argument; the dependency list, derivation and generated difference still require review.

## Earth and observer bounds

The 9,177 native Earth segments have degree 12 and eight-day spacing. Their binary64 coefficients are exact rationals in the derivation. On each segment, `|T′k(x)| ≤ k²` bounds each velocity component by `Σ k²|ak|/(width/2)`. Integer-square-root enclosures of the squared component bounds give vector speed. Exact one-day radius nodes, widened by half a day's segment speed, cover every point of each segment. Endpoint sums give the joins without sampling between them. The final partly used segment is bounded in full.

The resulting ecliptic bounds are speed at most 0.018707485752318148 AU/day, radius at least 0.9740659185179674 AU and at most 1.0256170190798852 AU, largest join at most 2.1911251195548978e-13 AU, and total joins at most 2.8421338303643e-10 AU. The fixed EQJ matrix is not assumed to be perfectly orthogonal: rational bounds on its Gram matrix transform these quantities. The observer radius is at most `(6378.1366 + 10)/149597870.700` AU. A further proved margin in the topocentric denominator covers the composed time perturbations.

A separate, coarse Clenshaw/normalization/fixed-matrix rounding calculation verifies that the actual native radius, its norm and division rounding cannot backdate by more than 0.006 day. This guard is used to enclose the runtime path. Its polynomial/frame error is not added to the public partial altitude budget. Polynomial fitting error relative to VSOP87B, and model error relative to independent ephemerides, remain different quantities.

## Time arithmetic and classification

The arithmetic arguments use IEEE 754 binary64 round-to-nearest with `u = 2^-53` and `γn = nu/(1−nu)`, without unsafe floating-point reassociation. Correctly rounded square roots and exact reduction modulo one are the arithmetic assumptions used where stated. Other math-library and frame-operation errors remain excluded from the altitude budget.

The civil bound covers Foundation's epoch-offset addition and the subtraction/division forming civil days. Civil UTC conversion includes the native affine table expression and its decimal literals. The TT inverse bound starts from the actual native residual tolerance `max(1e-12, 2 × Double.ulpOfOne × |tt|)`, includes forward-TT rounding, and divides by a proved positive lower slope. The source-derived smooth Delta T slope bounds use the actual Gregorian-year denominators. Across a model jump, the inverse modulus includes the enclosed absolute jump sum; a smooth derivative bound alone is insufficient.

Classification has its own guard. Over UT `[-37000,37000]`, exact Gregorian year starts and the native upper-year clamp keep a fixed supplied UT in the correct integer-year polynomial piece. An outward expression majorant, derivative bound, multiplication graph and rounding-factor count give Delta T evaluation error below `1e-10` second and complete native forward-TT error below `5e-12` day. Runtime classification uses the looser `1e-9`-day guard. This does not add Delta T evaluation error to the public altitude budget. Rounded backdating or inverse UT can still cross a model boundary, so differing UT arguments must use both one-sided values or the absolute jump allowance.

The runtime first encloses both arrival UT values, including inverse/civil uncertainty and the classification guard. It enlarges the interval by the proved maximum delay, then encloses its TT image with outward endpoint arithmetic and the absolute sum of enclosed model jumps. Downward jumps make unexpanded endpoint images insufficient. The initial TT values are included too. Every boundary contact is treated conservatively. The whole hull must remain in the Earth polynomial domain before a bound is returned.

Positive-gap limits are derived from the exact one-sided polynomials and converted to the correct binary64 endpoint classifications. A civil input whose conversion interval can intersect such a gap is also refused. Civil dates within the derived calendar uncertainty of a civil-table segment start remain unsupported. Nonfinite inputs and invalid observers retain their explicit errors; the observer budget is limited to 10 km above or below the ellipsoid.

## Finite light-time return

A global smooth contraction claim is false for this model. At UT 1826.5056790659519, backdating can round across the 2005 Delta T boundary: native and extended-precision arithmetic can stop on different iterations, with an EQJ direction difference about 5.90e-7 degree. The evidence retains that discrepancy. There are also exact-real cycling arrival intervals at negative model steps, although their nearest tested public scalar inputs return. Those real-input cycles cannot be dismissed by increasing precision.

For a proof, write `g(q)=q+ΔT(q)/86400` and `E(x)` for the exact EQJ Earth vector. On an enclosing domain, use `|g(q)−g(r)| ≤ a|q−r|+D` and `|E(x)−E(z)| ≤ V|x−z|+P`, with absolute jump sums `D` and `P`. Set `k=aV/c`. Successive exact UT iterates satisfy `Bn = k^(n−1)T + ((VD+P)/c)(1−k^(n−1))/(1−k)`. The source/data-checked sufficient constants `a≤1.001`, `V≤0.019`, `D≤2.5e-6` day, `P≤1e-9` AU and `T≤0.006` day give `aB10 < 1e-9` day.

Failure to return by the tenth evaluation must therefore cross a Delta T jump in its last UT step. For each one-sided boundary value `b`, the arrival UT lies in a closed strip centered on `b+|E(g(b±))|/c`, with halfwidth `(1+k)B10+P/c`, less than 2.86e-10 day. Rational enclosures construct these strips, map them separately to binary64 UT, binary64 TT and Foundation Date scalar lattices, and prove separation from arrival inverse/civil boundaries. Cases at 1900 are excluded by the required Earth-domain containment, not by an unexplained omission.

The checked artifact covers 49,888 candidate incidences in 66 model/side/input-scale strips, using 364 interval batches. Incidences can overlap; they are not a claim of 49,888 distinct times. Every batch proves a strict successful TT stopping inequality by evaluation ten. The largest final residual enclosure is below 8.714e-10 day. An unresolved singleton would fail the certificate. A regression deliberately keeps a nonrepresentable real cycling input unresolved, while certifying its representable neighbor. This finite argument establishes return for the supported public scalar references; measurements alone do not.

## Returned-iterate error and sensitivities

For two returned iterates with TT coordinates `x,z`, let `sN,sR` bound their exact stopping residuals, `ηg` forward-TT arithmetic, `ηq` norm/division/light-speed-literal/UT-subtraction arithmetic, and `δu` the arrival-UT discrepancy. With an Earth evaluation perturbation `ηE`, the triangle inequality gives `|x−z| ≤ [sN+sR+ηg+a(δu+ηq+(P+ηE)/c)+D]/(1−k)`. Returned-vector discrepancy is at most `V|x−z|+P+ηE`. This comparison needs neither equal stop indices nor a unique fixed point. Both stopping tolerances contribute. The preceding finite certificate supplies the otherwise missing exact-reference return premise.

The public partial term sets the excluded Earth/frame/math perturbations to zero; their propagation through the iteration remains excluded as well. A polynomial-join allowance is included even for a point whose backdating interval might straddle a join. A separate model-step supplement is added whenever the conservative backdating interval can reach a Delta T boundary. The held JPL model does not acquire a 2050 step it never evaluates.

The altitude sensitivities compose the geocentric vector modulus with the observer radius, the ERA rate, and source-derived rates of precession, mean obliquity, all nutation terms, all complementary terms and the mean-sidereal polynomial. Polynomial rates use `Σ k|ck|T^(k−1)`; periodic rates include both amplitude derivatives and argument rates. The generated UT sensitivity is below 362.103 degrees/day and the TT sensitivity below 0.001125 degrees/day. The rational artifact records the individual terms and the positive topocentric denominator.

Baseline outward-rounded terms include TT inverse 8.9247138241513e-9 degree, light-time termination 2.227608200238734e-9 degree and ERA arithmetic 3.300004191721738e-11 degree. The civil construction adds its own terms. Near model boundaries, the generated conservative supplements can be larger: about 2.752e-6 degree for backdating across a step and 0.000906 degree for an inverse branch uncertainty. These are derived allowances, not fitted maxima. ``SolarAltitudeObservation/ErrorBound/total`` rounds every addition outward.

## Measurements and limits

Each full-altitude configuration records 1,320 canonical requests across both models and six observers, including polar locations, heights of ±10 km, coverage and fallback grids, adjacent polynomial/model seams, backdating iteration-flip neighborhoods, and civil inputs. The reports retain native bits, native/reference iteration traces, public observation budgets or unsupported reasons, exact-input references, source/data hashes, compiler identity and process measurements. The primitive reports separately retain their 162-row grids. Their known one-ULP Debug/Release nutation difference at the 2101 fallback sample remains evidence of configuration-dependent rounding, not a bit-identity promise between builds.

The full-altitude reports differ in 11 altitude results: ten supported rows differ by at most four ULP, and one excluded fallback row differs by eight ULP. Their sampled maxima are identical. Paired-altitude identity is asserted within a configuration, not between configurations.

These grids do not prove continuous polynomial, frame, sidereal, horizon or libm accuracy. Their maxima do not set new scientific thresholds. The paired-altitude identity is within one build and one recorded model/time pair. Raw evidence and reproduction commands are in `Scripts/solar-numerics/README.md`; production Release build/artifact observations and measurement-process latency/RSS are reported separately on the PR. No local measurement establishes another architecture's libm accuracy or library-only retained memory.
