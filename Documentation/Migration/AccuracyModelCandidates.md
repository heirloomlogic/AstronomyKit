# Accuracy model candidates

Research date: 2026-10-03. This note evaluates bounded repair candidates against the owner's settled targets: 1900–2130 TT inclusive, angular error at most one arcminute, event-time error strictly below 60 seconds, and matched distance error at most one part per million. The current production solar model remains selected. These are requirements to qualify, not claims that any candidate passes. See [AccuracyAcceptanceDecisions.md](AccuracyAcceptanceDecisions.md).

## Why these candidates

The frozen distance results retrospectively imply sampled relative errors of approximately 39.6 ppm for the Moon, 34.9 ppm for Pluto, 3.3 ppm for Uranus and 2.3 ppm for Neptune. Those measurements used the historical 1900–2100 archive and its matched observables; they do not newly validate the target interval or quantify reference uncertainty. [DistanceAccuracyEvidence.md](DistanceAccuracyEvidence.md) retains the underlying results and limits. [ModelFitnessDiagnosis.md](ModelFitnessDiagnosis.md) separates arithmetic, coefficient, integration and historical-reference effects: improving arithmetic alone does not resolve the sampled Uranus/Neptune discrepancy, and a smaller Pluto integration step alone does not resolve its seed/reference mismatch.

At a matched lunar range of 384,000 km, 1 ppm permits 0.384 km. This arithmetic makes a modern lunar trajectory or improved full lunar series worth evaluating. It does not permit substituting angular agreement for radial accuracy, or radial agreement for the owner's event-time target.

Feasibility conclusion: one-ppm distance accuracy is plausible with a different scientific model, and the lunar literature below provides a concrete example of much tighter model agreement on a shorter interval. The existing model does not meet it on its frozen Moon/Pluto/Uranus/Neptune samples. It is therefore a reasonable replacement-model target, but it would be an unsupported promise for the present compact model or for the untested 1900–2130 interval. Choosing a looser compact-model product policy would be a separate owner decision, not a reinterpretation of the historical acceptance archive.

DE442 was integrated in May 2024 principally to improve the Uranus barycenter for development of URA182, adding Uranus occultations and further Mars-orbiter/Juno ranging data. It is a concrete Uranus-sensitive alternative to test, rather than a general accuracy guarantee for every body. The inspected comments provide no uniform one-ppm promise for our interval. [Official DE442 technical comments](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/planets/de442_tech-comments.txt).

## Centers are a required part of the model

Both short planetary distributions contain the Sun, planetary-system barycenters 1–9, Mercury/Venus body centers, and Moon 301/Earth 399 relative to Earth-Moon barycenter 3. A geometric geocentric Moon state can therefore be obtained as `state(301,3) − state(399,3)` at one common epoch. They do not contain Uranus 799, Neptune 899 or Pluto 999 body-center offsets; their 7/8/9 states cannot silently replace public body-center semantics. [Official planetary SPK summaries](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/planets/aa_summaries.txt).

Satellite kernels supply central-planet mass-center states relative to their system barycenters and often include planetary/Sun/Earth trajectories as well. Candidate center corrections with sufficient published coverage are below. [NAIF satellite-kernel explanation](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/satellites/AAREADME_Satellite_SPKs), [official satellite summaries](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/satellites/aa_summaries.txt).

| Body-center correction | Published interval | Useful constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uranus 799 relative to 7 in `ura184_part-3.bsp` | 1600-01-04 through 2399-12-17 ET | Covers the target; parts 1/2 end in January 2100 and are insufficient through 2130. |
| Neptune 899 relative to 8 in `nep097.bsp` | 1600-01-10 through 2399-12-31 ET | Covers the target; inspect the source fit and embedded DE states before combining. |

URA184 part 3 explicitly embeds DE442 Uranus/Sun/Earth states. PLU060 embeds DE440 states and identifies a DE440/LE440 fit, with a stated 1.7-metre Chebyshev interpolation error. NEP097's merged planetary states are from DE440 while its integration metadata identify DE441/LE441. These labels must remain recorded: interpolation error is not total physical ephemeris uncertainty, and combining trajectories requires an explicit reference contract. [URA184 comments](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/satellites/ura184_part-3.cmt), [PLU060 comments](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/satellites/plu060.cmt), [NEP097 comments](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/satellites/nep097.cmt).

Engineering consequence: select and hash the exact barycenter and center-offset segments, rather than blindly loading overlapping kernels and letting load order choose the scientific solution. A targeted derived resource may be much smaller than the multi-body satellite distribution; its exact size remains unmeasured. Preserve the current solar API model while explicitly treating DE Sun/Earth states, if required for coordinate or received-light evaluation, as auxiliary source states. Mixing a DE target with existing solar/Earth helpers still requires matched geometric/apparent and frame/time validation.

## Improved analytical Moon alternative

ELP/MPP02 is a plausible full-series lunar candidate. The original Chapront/Francou paper reports a maximum radial discrepancy below 2.5 metres against DE405 after fitting constants over 1950–2060, with about 0.5-metre RMS, and improved angular coordinates compared with the earlier ELP series. Its century-fit evidence is much tighter than the arithmetic 0.384-km lunar allowance, but refers to that older source and restricted interval. It cannot be transferred to a port, truncation, or the complete 1900–2130 target without fresh measurements and convention alignment. [Original ELP/MPP02 paper](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2003/23/aa3101.pdf).

The [official IMCCE ELP2000-82B distribution](https://ftp.imcce.fr/pub/ephem/moon/elp82b/) and [CDS author-associated catalog](https://cdsarc.u-strasbg.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/VI/79?format=html&tex=true) provide older full lunar-series data/evaluator materials. They are provenance sources for comparison, not evidence that ELP2000-82B meets the settled requirements. Full ELP/MPP02 source acquisition, a shipping grant for the original coefficients and evaluator, modern-reference error through 2130, term-selection error, and measured Swift cost remain unresolved in this research. A permissive license on an unrelated port would not by itself establish rights in the original scientific data.

The analytical route offers an explicit lunar-theory representation and potential coefficient truncation, but introduces a second source family alongside the outer-planet repair. Truncation needs its own position, range and derivative budget; reducing its data size before establishing full-series fitness would confound theory mismatch with implementation approximation. These are engineering tradeoffs, not a selected production direction.

## Distribution and evaluator choices

NAIF permits commercial use of SPICE without fees, unmodified kernel redistribution, and integration of Toolkit modules into a customer-built tool. Redistribution of the complete unmodified Toolkit as a mirror requires written clearance. Modified kernels must use modifier attribution, and self-created/modified kernels require appropriate metadata and validation; keep the precise source history in our acquisition manifest. These specific terms apply to NAIF-distributed products, not an assumed MIT grant or an automatic grant for another distributor's data. [NAIF use and redistribution rules](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/naif/rules.html).

SPK Type 2 represents positions using fixed-interval Chebyshev coefficients and obtains velocity by differentiating those polynomials; Type 3 stores separate position and velocity coefficients. A narrow native evaluator can be considered only after enumerating the selected source segment types and retaining frame, target, center and TDB metadata. Source evaluation, extracted coefficients and a newly fitted approximation are different engineering options. [NAIF SPK format and evaluator documentation](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/C/req/spk.html).

| Bounded candidate | What it could repair | Cost or uncertainty to measure before selection |
| --- | --- | --- |

No achieved size or runtime is asserted. |

TOP2013 remains a diagnostic reference rather than an immediately shippable replacement: the prior inspection established its old INPOP10a fit, Pluto-Charon center and unresolved redistribution grant, and its sampled mismatch with modern references. Replacing the current custom integrator with full TOP2013 would not, by itself, repair that historical-reference difference. [ModelFitnessDiagnosis.md](ModelFitnessDiagnosis.md), [official TOP2013 description](https://www.imcce.fr/recherche/equipes/pegase/theories), [official TOP2013 distribution](https://ftp.imcce.fr/pub/ephem/planets/top2013/).

## Evidence to make the next choice concrete

The concrete owner tradeoff is whether a measured increase in bundled data buys the required accuracy and simpler consistent evaluation, versus maintaining a more compact analytical/integrated representation with additional scientific and numerical qualification. Selection of DE440 versus DE442 should follow body-specific comparisons and compatible center sources; neither version name nor rounded catalog size settles it. This note does not ask for a choice before those costs and candidate results exist.

Candidate evaluation should first freeze exact source hashes, center/frame/time/correction conventions and any approximation budget, then characterize the failing bodies over the complete target and preserve a separate holdout. Include boundary epochs, coefficient seams and light-time epochs outside the requested instant; evaluate radial/angular errors separately and solve the agreed event observables independently. A comparison against the same source used to generate coefficients validates reproduction/approximation, while a different source or observational evidence is needed to discuss scientific independence. Closely related DE versions share observations/dynamics and are not independent physical-truth measurements.

Position error does not guarantee event-time error, especially for slow polar/grazing crossings or a station where rate is close to zero. Body-count/direction disagreements, undefined events and inadequate reference precision must remain explicit. Retaining the selected solar model leaves its event qualification, time/orientation conventions and mixed-model effects visible; a lunar/outer-body repair does not silently close the solar or general event requirements.

Existing C-to-Swift representation, build, performance, mixed-workload memory, Linux and downstream integration gates remain separate. Catalog sizes and mathematical Chebyshev differentiation do not prove those gates. No candidate kernel was downloaded, no replacement selected, and no production implementation, frozen allowance or accepted threshold was changed in this research.
