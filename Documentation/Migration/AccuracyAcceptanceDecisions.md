# Accuracy acceptance decisions

These interactive owner decisions were recorded on 2026-10-03 for the independent accuracy work in #81 and the model-fitness follow-up diagnosed in #119. This contract selects angular position, general event-time and relative distance targets but does not select a replacement model, adopt a standalone rate limit, or assert that the required interval has been validated. [approved-accuracy-targets.json](approved-accuracy-targets.json) records the approved numerical requirements. Historical fixtures and their frozen allowances remain evidence of their original policies and scopes.

## Approved decisions

1. Retain the current production solar model after reviewing [the independent polar evidence](PolarSunriseIndependentEvidence.md). Keep #124 open with its archived row, known-failure assertion and unchanged 70.8-second allowance.
2. Require the distance/model-fitness accuracy target to cover calendar years 1900 through 2130, inclusive. Use the existing distance investigation's explicit TT convention: `1900-01-01T00:00:00 TT <= epoch < 2131-01-01T00:00:00 TT`. This target does not narrow the public API's accepted date domain or qualify the interval outside it.
3. Support both accurate planet positions/event times and distances reported as measurements, with positions/event times as the primary use. Direct distance measurements remain a real secondary requirement. Position, event-time, distance and rate acceptance must remain distinct rather than transferring one tolerance to another quantity.
4. Target at most one arcminute of angular position error for the Sun, Moon and planets, including Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, across 1900–2130. One arcminute is 1/60 degree; it is not a one-minute event-time allowance. This is a requirement to qualify, not a claim that the existing implementation meets it throughout the interval. Existing test allowances and historical source evidence are not changed by adopting this target.
5. Require strictly less than one minute of event-time error for lunar phase changes, equinoxes and solstices across 1900–2130: `abs(calculatedTT - independentReferenceTT) * 86400 < 60`. An error of exactly 60 seconds fails. Retain source time-scale and rounding conventions during independent comparison; this requirement does not establish future civil UTC conversion accuracy. Existing source-specific fixture limits remain intact, and this target needs separate qualification.
6. Apply the same strict `<60 seconds` target and inclusive 1900–2130 interval to planetary stations and all similar event-time accuracy decisions without asking the owner again. This includes conjunctions/oppositions, apsides, nodes, rise/set and eclipse/transit contacts and peaks when the event exists and its observable is consistently defined. Do not introduce unapproved looser allowances or silently omit difficult polar/grazing cases. Undefined events, event-count/direction disagreements, unmatched observables and inadequate reference precision must be reported explicitly rather than counted as passing timing comparisons. The owner's instruction is to ask only about materially different requirements; the existing one-arcminute angular target remains distinct from the clock-minute target.
7. Initially select a uniform 1 ppm reported-distance target, then supersede it after reviewing current-model feasibility with these body-specific targets across 1900–2130: 1 ppm for Sun/Earth/Mercury/Venus/Mars/Jupiter/Saturn, 10 ppm for Uranus/Neptune, and 100 ppm for Moon/Pluto. Require `abs(calculatedDistance - referenceDistance) <= epsilonBody * referenceDistance`, where `epsilonBody` is the approved fractional limit. These are owner-selected, evidence-informed engineering reporting requirements, not published scientific bounds or qualified continuous-domain guarantees. Source-model uncertainty, observable alignment, numerical approximation and independent sampling remain separate evidence requirements; the historical 2× regression budgets remain unchanged.

## Remaining decisions

Position, event-time and reported-distance targets and the date interval are settled; do not repeat those questions. A standalone direct-rate accuracy limit remains unassigned; event-time rates must be qualified against the approved event timing and derivative convention. Existing direct-rate residual observations remain diagnostics outside that qualified use rather than blocking position/event/distance investigation on an arbitrary new velocity budget. Model selection or an API-contract change remains a distinct decision if necessary.

Preserve existing public center-of-body semantics and each API's declared geometric/received-light and frame conventions when constructing comparisons; resolve reference differences explicitly rather than choosing a better-matching target after observing residuals. These existing API contracts guide the qualification work without requesting a redundant policy choice. A candidate model change that cannot satisfy them must be surfaced as a distinct decision. Once radial and angular limits are defined for consistently matched vectors, a sufficient three-dimensional error budget can be derived from those limits rather than asking the owner to guess a separate arbitrary kilometre value. Direct rate accuracy outside the approved event-time use still needs an explicit requirement or an evidence-based proposal.

The approved angular target follows Astronomy Engine's documented one-arcminute design target. Existing local JPL tests generally use one arcminute but allow 1.5 arcminutes for Uranus, Neptune and Pluto. The uniform target requires new qualification evidence, not relabeling those existing tests as proof. [Upstream design target](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy#overview), [current JPL test tolerances](../../Tests/AstronomyKitTests/JPLValidationTests.swift).

Planetary station qualification uses the sign-changing zero of apparent Earth-centered ecliptic longitude rate under an explicitly matched correction/frame/time convention. Position accuracy and local agreement between an analytic rate and a finite difference do not establish independent station-time accuracy; convention-complete independent timing evidence is required.

For consistently matched vectors, the radial and angular targets provide a sufficient three-dimensional error budget without another arbitrary owner-selected kilometre value. If `r` is the reference norm, `epsilon` is that body's approved relative radial limit, and `alpha = pi/10800` radians (one arcminute), the triangle inequality gives `vectorError <= r * (epsilon + 2 * (1 + epsilon) * sin(alpha/2))`. This is a mathematical consequence conditional on both approved limits and matching origin/epoch/frame/observable; it does not prove either limit or apply to unmatched historical vector residuals.

[The retrospective distance assessment](ApprovedDistanceRetrospective.md) applies the approved body-specific targets to the existing source-bound samples without changing the old budgets. The prior holdout is not a newly independent acceptance set for these later-selected requirements. Its nominal non-exceedances are finite observations, not whole-domain qualification or authority to change a production model.

After initially approving 1 ppm, the owner requested realistic expectations rather than blocking on an impractical requirement, then explicitly approved the body-specific revision in [DistanceAccuracyRealism.md](DistanceAccuracyRealism.md). The machine-readable policy and current retrospective assessment now use the revised limits. The former uniform-policy bytes, report and evaluator snapshot are retained locally under `.context/accuracy-policy-history/`; the original frozen distance characterization/allowances/acceptance remain intact in their original locations. No angular/time requirement was weakened by this distance revision.
