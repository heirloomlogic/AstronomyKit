Earth Rotation - the Change in the Length of Day and ΔT

ΔT & lod from −2000 to +2500

This resource provides the results of the modern (updated in 2020) analysis of eclipse and occultation timings from ancient Babylonian, China, and the Arab and European worlds through early telescope observations to modern observations in the following papers:

  • Measurement of the Earth's rotation: 720 BC to AD 2015, published in 2016 in the Royal Society's Proceedings A 472, and made freely available via Open Access, by Stephenson, F.R., Morrison, L.V. and Hohenkerk, C.Y..

and updated in

  • Addendum 2020 to `Measurement of the Earth's Rotation: 720 BC to AD 2015', published in 2021 February in the Royal Society's Proceedings A 478, by Morrison, L. V., Stephenson, F.R., Hohenkerk, C.Y. and M. Zawilski, M..

Download the file of FAQs to get some general information about the paper.

The analysis provides measurement of the rotation of the Earth and allows formation of the change in the length of day (lod), relative to the standard day of 86400 SI seconds, and ΔT(t), the difference (using current terminology) between TT and UT1, via a spline fit using a weighted least-squares analysis of the data.

The values of ΔT in the tables listed in the various sections should be used in conjunction with lunar ephemeris designated JPL DE430/LE430 in which the tidal acceleration of the Moon is −25.85"/cy2.

The menus below allows users to access all the data used in this Addendum 2020, which consists of both new and updated data as well the data from the original 2016 Royal Society paper.

Observational Data and Spline Polynomial Coefficients for ΔT - Version 2020

This section provides links of the various data files (ASCII) relevant to the paper. These files are also available from the data tab of the Royal Society Proceedings A website.

Tables 1-9, 12-14 are ASCII versions of the tables published in the 2016 paper, and Tables 10 v2020 and 11 v2020, are updated in the Addendum 2020, covering years BC 720 to 1605 AD. Each file table contains a few explanatory records at the end of each file.

The extract from the Lunar Occultation Archive VI/132B, (Herald+ 2012) held at by the Centre de Données Astronomiques de Strasbourg (CDS) archive covers years 1621 to 2015, and the format is described in the ReadMe file (see below).

The values of ΔT for the final three years, 2016.0 to 2019.0, are taken from the published values in The Astronomical Almanac which are from the IERS Bulletin B.

The spline approximation is provided via a set of polynomial coefficients (Table S15 v2020).

ΔT and length of day (lod) from −2000 to −800

  • Summary

  • lod

The values tabulated in the ΔT and lod tables have been extrapolated backwards by integration using the long term lod function

lod = +1.72 t − 3.5 sin(2π(t+0.75)/14)

between the years −800 to −2000, where t is the number of centuries from +1825.0.

The resulting green-dashed line in the plot shows the extrapolated ΔT, while the continuous-black line shows the spline.

For ΔT the values are tabulated in hours every 100 years to the nearest 100 seconds.

ΔT and length of day (lod) from +1950 to +2019

  • Summary
  • ΔT
  • lod

The most accurate results from 1962 onwards are those derived from VLBI and other high-accuracy observations. These are available from the International Earth Rotation and Reference System Service (IERS).

The less accurate ΔT and lod listed here are evaluated from a spline fit to the occultation observations, which has the effect of smoothing out the annual and sub-annual fluctuations in the raw data. The values are tabulated at half-yearly intervals to a precision of 0.01 seconds in ΔT and 0.1 ms in lod. They agree to within ±0.05 seconds with the IERS values of ΔT. Taken together with the occultation data before 1962, the results tabulated here show the long-term trends in these Earth's rotation parameters.
