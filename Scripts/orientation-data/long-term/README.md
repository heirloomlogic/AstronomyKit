# Long-term precession

`Engine.Precession` uses the unbiased Vondrák, Capitaine & Wallace model from [2011](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201117274), including the [2012 corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201117274e), as implemented by ERFA 2.0.1. `manifest.json` pins the unmodified C sources, headers and published tests at commit `9915ba38c9365f8b0738269b8c2ac1fdd5f8dee3`. Each file retains its license. The package ships the generated coefficients and Swift implementation; it neither compiles nor calls this C archive.

Run `python3 Scripts/generate-long-term-precession.py` to regenerate the Swift coefficients and `python3 Scripts/generate-long-term-precession-fixtures.py` to compile the archived C oracle in a temporary directory and capture reference matrices. Both support `--check`; the fixture check allows 5e-16 absolute element variation for cross-platform libm rounding. Runtime fixture tolerances remain 1e-15 for captured matrices and 1e-14 for ERFA’s published matrix and pole values. The latter are literal values from the archived `t_erfa_c.c`. The independent observational archives and their tolerances are unchanged.

EQJ remains the mean equator and equinox of J2000. The runtime follows `eraLtp`, not the ICRS-biased `eraLtpb`; fixtures retain both to catch a bias substitution. The equator pole `q` and ecliptic pole `p` define matrix rows `x = normalize(q × p)`, `y = q × x`, and `z = q`. Mean obliquity is `atan2(|q × p|, q · p)`. The fixed EQJ-to-J2000-ecliptic tilt remains 84381.406 arcseconds. Finite decimal coefficients leave ERFA’s J2000 matrix element `[2][1] = 4.848244276876856e-12` rad, about one microarcsecond, with the opposite sign in `[1][2]`; this published model residual is retained and tested, without epoch renormalization or frame bias.

The public body acceptance remains TT ±1461000 days. Date-frame rotations use the long-term model throughout that interval, including exact endpoints and adjacent interior doubles. Rotation factories retain their existing behavior outside body acceptance: they do not impose a separate finite-date range guard, and nonfinite TT propagates NaN elements. Far-epoch published pole tests may exercise the internal mathematical model beyond public body acceptance; they do not extend the public ephemeris range. Nutation remains IAU 2006/2000A, and ERA/GMST retain their existing IAU definitions. Apparent sidereal time uses the new mean obliquity in its existing equation of the equinoxes. This composition is not a claim that every orientation component has independently established physical accuracy over ±4000 years.

Run `python3 Scripts/qualify-long-term-precession.py --check` to reconstruct `qualification.json`. It covers every real TT in the accepted interval using 320 closed quarter-century boxes, and separately covers the altitude certificate’s ±1.02-century enclosure. `precession_bounds.py` uses rational Machin bounds for π, outward interval arithmetic, Taylor sine/cosine polynomials with explicit next-derivative remainder bounds, and integer-square-root enclosures. It rejects a nonpositive pole radicand or equinox cross-product norm. These are bounds on the exact-decimal coefficient model; they exclude floating-point evaluation and physical model error.

For a pole `(a,b,sqrt(1−a²−b²))`, its derivative norm is at most `hypot(a′,b′)/sqrt(1−a²−b²)`. Harmonic derivative majorants use `ω(|C|+|S|)` and polynomial derivatives use `Σ k|ck|T^(k−1)`. Constant ecliptic-pole rotation preserves that norm. The cross derivative is bounded by the sum of pole derivative norms. For the normalized equinox, the orthogonal projection in `x′ = (v′−x(x·v′))/|v|` cannot increase length. Combining the three row derivative bounds in a Frobenius norm bounds the matrix operator derivative. The angle between the two unit poles changes no faster than the sum of their angular speeds. Conversion to degrees per TT day is outward. No sampled maximum is used to establish these bounds.

Across ±40 centuries, the rational artifact encloses the equator radicand above 0.8619107, the ecliptic radicand above 0.9999125 and the cross-product norm above 0.3871389. The matrix derivative norm is below 0.000126022 degrees/day. These regularity margins cover direct date-frame evaluation; ephemeris retarded-time coverage remains the separate body-specific contract. The altitude derivation uses its narrower ±1.02-century enclosure and does not extend the existing altitude certificate’s coverage.
